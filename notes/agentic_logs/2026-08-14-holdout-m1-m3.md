# Holding M1 and M3 out of GiftEvalPretrain (2026-08-14)

## Why

The evaluation plan uses M1, M3, M4, and the GIFT-Eval benchmark as test
sets. Of those, M1 and M3 ship inside GiftEvalPretrain, so training on the
full corpus would be training on test.

Audit of the local corpus (152 directories under
`/zfsauton/scratch/istepka/lts/data/giftevalpretrain_full`) against
GIFT-Eval's own `notebooks/dataset_properties.json` (28 configs / 23
datasets):

- M1 present: `m1_yearly`, `m1_quarterly`, `m1_monthly`
- M3 present: `monash_m3_{yearly,quarterly,monthly,other}`
- M4 absent, consistent with the corpus card's no-leakage claim
- No GIFT-Eval benchmark dataset appears by name

Two source-level near-overlaps were checked rather than assumed:

- `beijing_air_quality` / `china_air_quality` vs bench `kdd_cup_2018`
  (KDD Cup 2018 is Beijing/London air quality). Both are already absent
  from the context-512 index, so this is moot in practice.
- `weather` vs bench `jena_weather`. Fingerprinted directly: pretrain
  `weather` is 3010 univariate daily series (`T1`..`T3010`, 4225 points,
  starting 1970, the Monash Australian weather set); `jena_weather` is a
  single 21-channel multivariate item of 366 days starting 2020. Different
  datasets, so `weather` stays in pretraining.

Tourism and Favorita were subsequently added as test sets in their own
right (they are not part of GIFT-Eval, whose Sales datasets are
`car_parts`, `restaurant`, and Mancuso et al.'s Italian grocery
`hierarchical_sales`), so `tourism_{yearly,quarterly,monthly}`,
`favorita_sales`, and `favorita_transactions` are held out too. Favorita is
the first held-out dataset that actually contributes training windows.

## Canonical eval copies

The GiftEvalPretrain copies of M1 and Tourism are incomplete, so they must
not be used as evaluation data:

| Suite | corpus copy | canonical | missing |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | 921 | 1001 | 80 (yearly 106/181, quarterly 198/203) |
| M3 | 3003 | 3003 | none |
| Tourism | 1212 | 1311 | 99 (yearly 419/518) |

Canonical `.tsf` copies of M1, M3, and Tourism were downloaded from the
Monash Zenodo records into
`/zfsauton/scratch/istepka/lts/data/monash_eval/` and verified at 1001 /
3003 / 1311 series. Evaluation reads from there, never from the held-out
corpus directories.

M4 is evaluated from the official competition data at
`/zfsauton/scratch/istepka/lts/data/m4_official/`, downloaded from
Mcompetitions/M4-methods. All 100,000 series are present with the official
train/test split and competition horizons:

| | series | horizon |
|---|---|---|
| Yearly | 23,000 | 6 |
| Quarterly | 24,000 | 8 |
| Monthly | 48,000 | 18 |
| Weekly | 359 | 13 |
| Daily | 4,227 | 14 |

The gift-eval checkout's `m4_yearly` holds only 22,974 series, dropping 26
under GIFT-Eval's own preprocessing. That copy stays in use for the
GIFT-Eval benchmark run, where matching the leaderboard's distribution is
the point, but the standalone M4 suite uses the official data so results
are comparable to the M4 literature. The two are not reconcilable by id:
GIFT-Eval renumbers series positionally (`0`, `1`, ...) while the official
data uses `Y1`, `Y2`, ...

GIFT-Eval itself comes from the repo checkout at
`/zfsauton/scratch/istepka/lts/data/gift-eval` (all 23 datasets / 28
configs present).

## Favorita eval protocol

Favorita has no canonical series count, so the corpus copy is adopted as
the definition: 111,840 store-item daily series, 139.2M points, plus 54
store-level `favorita_transactions` series. It matches the Kaggle
competition's span, ending 2017-08-15.

The Kaggle competition's own test period is the 16 days 2017-08-16 to
2017-08-31, whose labels were never released, so the horizon length is
reused against data we hold:

- **Horizon**: the last 16 days, 2017-07-31 to 2017-08-15.
- **Eligibility**: every series reaching 2017-08-15, whatever its history
  length. That is 83,207 of 111,840 series (74%). No minimum context is
  imposed: the eval follows the competition setup, where each store-item
  pair is forecast from whatever history it has, rather than being shaped
  around any one model's context window.
- **Missing values**: 20.5% of points are NaN because Kaggle omits rows for
  days with no sale. Kaggle's stated convention is to read those as zero
  sales, so NaN is filled with 0 rather than masked. Note this differs from
  the pretraining path, which drops windows below `min_valid_fraction`.

History lengths among eligible series run 250 to 1688 days (median 1355,
1st percentile 262). 6,956 of them (8.4%) hold fewer than the 512 points a
full model context wants, so the harness must left-pad or mask those rather
than drop them. None are pathologically short.

Alternatives considered and rejected: requiring 512 + 16 points drops
6,956 series for one model's convenience and bakes an architecture
constraint into the benchmark definition; requiring the full 2013-01-01
span keeps only 29,274 series (26%), discarding three quarters of the data
to buy calendar alignment the fixed end date already provides. Per-series
relative cutoffs were rejected because they test different calendar
periods per series.

Series that end before 2017-08-15 (28,633 of them, discontinued items)
have no observations in the horizon and are out of scope, matching the
competition, whose test set lists only pairs expected to sell in the test
window.

## Leakage audit against GIFT-Eval

Name matching is not sufficient, so all 53 datasets in the rebuilt index
were fingerprinted against all 55 GIFT-Eval configs on (series count,
start date, series length, frequency). **Zero suspicious pairs.** Two
specific early suspicions resolved:

- `solar_power` is a single 4-second series starting 2019-08-01; bench
  `solar` is 137 Alabama PV plants at 10min/hourly starting 2006-01-01.
- `weather` vs `jena_weather`, resolved above.

`beijing_air_quality` and `china_air_quality` (the `kdd_cup_2018` source
concern) are both absent from the index entirely, their series being too
short for the window length.

## Practical impact

At `context_length=512` + `prediction_length=128` a series needs 640 valid
points. Every M1, M3, and Tourism series is far shorter, so those ten
directories contribute zero of the 41,030,616 windows in
`outputs/gifteval_window_index/context512_pred128.parquet` (which covers
only 55 of the 152 directories); excluding them is a formality that would
start to matter if the context were ever shortened.

Favorita is different: `favorita_sales` contributes 77,891 windows and
`favorita_transactions` 143, together 78,034 windows or 0.19% of the
index. `resolve_window_index` raises on the old index rather than training
against it.

The index was rebuilt as job 31052 (COMPLETED, 41 min, 41.5GB peak RSS)
into `outputs/gifteval_window_index/context512_pred128_heldout.parquet`:
40,952,582 windows over 53 datasets (36,851,623 train / 4,100,959 val),
exactly 78,034 fewer than the old index and containing none of the 12
held-out datasets. The old index is left in place so completed runs stay
reproducible.

One caveat when comparing across the two indexes: `_dataset_scale_group`
splits the stable-hash-ordered dataset list at `len // 2`, so going from 55
to 53 datasets moves the boundary. Exactly one dataset changes arm,
`nn5_daily_with_missing` (verified against both built indexes, new group
sizes 26/27). Scale-swap results from the old and new indexes should not be
pooled without noting this.

## Change

`corpus.exclude` in `conf/tsfm_base.yaml`, holding the twelve M1, M3,
Tourism, and Favorita directories.

- `gc.discover_dataset_dirs(root, exclude)` filters them out and raises on
  an exclude name that is not present under the corpus root, so a typo
  cannot silently reinstate the leakage.
- `resolve_window_index` applies the list whether `corpus.datasets` is
  explicit or null, and **raises** if a loaded `cache_path` index contains
  an excluded dataset rather than filtering the table. Filtering after load
  would renumber `WindowIndex._dataset_scale_group`, which is derived from
  the dataset set and must stay stable across paired A/B runs. The fix is
  to rebuild the index.
- `build_gifteval_window_index.py --exclude` mirrors the config, and
  `build_gifteval_window_index.sbatch` defaults to the same twelve names.

`src/data/gifteval/audit.py` deliberately still fingerprints the whole
corpus: the audit describes what is on disk, not what is trained on.
